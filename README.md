# My Day – To-Do & Journal 📔📝

<img src="MyDay/Assets.xcassets/AppIcon.appiconset/AppIcon-1024.png" width="160" alt="My Day app icon" align="right">

A colourful, cosy iPhone app for planning the day, choosing what matters most,
and keeping a private journal. It is built with **SwiftUI** and **SwiftData**.

## Features

| Screen | What you can do |
| --- | --- |
| **My Day** (home) | The illustrated home screen from the design. The girl, pets and garden are an image; everything else is native SwiftUI: the ☰ menu, the "My Day" title, today's real date, the 🔔 reminders button, **Daily Motivation** (a short hand-written quote from 112 built-in quotes about happiness, productivity, gratitude, self-belief, consistency, peaceful living and progress; it stays the same all day, changes after midnight and works offline), the three feature cards with live badges, the pink **+** Quick Add menu and the floating tab bar. |
| **Today's To-Dos** | The illustrated header, today's date with a daily quote, category chips (All, Personal, Work, Health, Learning, Shopping, then your own) with a ＋ chip to add a category of your own (a name, an emoji and a colour; press and hold it to delete it, and its to-dos move to Personal), and soft pastel rows; chips and rows are in their category's colour (Personal pink, Work blue, Health green, Learning yellow, Shopping purple; categories you add are pink unless you pick another colour), rows such as "Health · 5:00 PM 🔔". The box on the left ticks a to-do off; the ☆ next to the ✏️ marks it important, and important to-dos (★) are always listed first; the little ✏️ on the right opens a menu: Edit, Change date/time, Add reminder, Repeat, Move to Priority and Delete. Swipe a to-do to the left to delete it, tap it for its details, or press and hold to drag it to a new place. Finished to-dos slide under a colourful **› Completed** button (with their count) below the list; it starts closed, and a tap shows or hides them. Ticking a to-do pops two little pink hearts out of its box, one a bit bigger and one very small, which float up and fade, and when every to-do is done a big shower of pink, purple, yellow and blue stars, hearts and confetti bursts across the screen (both skipped with Reduce Motion). **Today's Progress** (the counts, a heart for every to-do and the kitten's cheer, "All Done!" once every to-do is ticked) is pinned at the bottom, under the add buttons: the one place the page shows how much is done. Finishing a repeating to-do creates the next one. |
| **Four ways to add** | The two fastest are bright and glowing: a long pink ＋ **Add Task** button and, beside it, a round yellow 🎙 microphone labelled "Speak a Task". **Photo** (with the purple camera from its design: hearts, sparkles and a heart in the lens; the same camera stands in for every 📷 and 📸 in the app) and ▦ **Template** are smaller and softer, underneath. All four stay pinned at the bottom of the screen on a slim frosted panel, with Today's Progress as one very thin line at the very bottom ("1 of 16", a heart for every to-do and the cheer); the tab bar (My Day · Calendar · Insights · Settings) stays on the main screens and hides on To-Dos, so the list has the screen; only the list scrolls, sliding behind them. At the top, a strip of the "Today's To-Dos" picture stays fixed, clear and sharp (no blur, no bar over it), and the list starts right under it on a rounded panel. ＋ **Add Task**: a pastel sheet where only the title is needed; category, date & time, reminder, repeat, photo and note are optional. 🎙 **Speak a Task**: Apple's Speech framework (on the device when supported) turns what you say into text; My Day spots words like *today*, *tomorrow*, *at 5 PM* and *every day*; the to-do goes to the category chip you have chosen above (Health, Shopping or your own), or to a category you name ("… in my shopping list"), and with **All** chosen it is guessed from words like *doctor* or *milk* (Personal otherwise). It then shows your new to-do ready to change right there: the task (type over it), the category chips, and Date & Time, Reminder and Repeat rows that open in place, with **Add Task** (and "Add a photo or a note" for the full sheet). Nothing is saved without your tap, and guesses are pointed out. To-Dos has three one-time tips from their designs, over a soft pink shade (they come gently, on separate visits; see **Tips, gently** below): first the day's quote at the top glows in a bright frame with hearts, a two-headed pink arrow joins it to a card where the girl and her puppy lie reading (“A New Quote Every Day!”, with **Got it** and **Show me**, which makes the quote glow); then the girl and her puppy peek over a card pointing at the glowing 🎙 (“Add your task with your voice!”, with the example “Call Mom tomorrow at 5 PM.”, **Got it** and **Try it now**). Each tip pops up with My Day's discovery sound, “Ting… twinkle!” (0.7 s): one soft crystal “ting” and three tiny rising sparkles; the quote tip's version ends dreamier (“ting ✨ ting-ling ✨”). Then a third tip, as in its design: the girl and her puppy peek over a cloud that says “Add photos to your tasks! Tap here to add a photo.”, and a pink arrow points down at the glowing **📷 Photo** button (tap Photo to add a photo to-do, or anywhere else to close it). **Tips, gently**: every first-time tip in My Day is paced so it is read, not tapped away: none in the first minute of the very first use; a screen's tip waits 2.5 seconds after the screen opens, and a screen shows only one per visit, its most useful first (on To-Dos: Speak a Task, then Photo, then the quote; on a journal page: the moods, then Get a Prompt); after a tip the next waits at least 3 minutes, so there is time to try what it showed (30 seconds for a tip about something just tapped, like Quick Add or the ⋮ menu's Journal Theme); and at most 3 tips a day. Tips with their own timing (the Journal Lock tip on the first opening of the journal on a later day than its very first, and once more 7 days after Maybe Later) follow the same pacing. None shows again once seen (until **Settings → Tips → Show tips again**, which brings them all back and opens To-Dos; since they were asked for, there is no waiting then: a screen's tips come one right after another, until each has shown again). The first time a to-do's **Task Details** opens (one without a reminder), the sheet rises to full height and a one-time tip from its design points at **🔔 Reminder**: the row glows white with a pink edge and yellow rays at its top corners, the puppy with pink bows peeks over a cream cloud saying “🔔 Task Reminders · Don’t let an important task slip away 💕 · Add a reminder and My Day will help you remember.”, and a pink arrow curls from the cloud up to the row (tap the row to open the reminder, or anywhere else to close the tip; it waits for another to-do when the cloud would not fit under the row). Categories: Personal, Work, Health, Learning and Shopping, then the person's own (made with ＋, kept on their iPhone and in their iCloud only), then Entertainment, Hobbies, Finance and Appointments; each shows its cute picture from the Task Categories designs in the tabs, Task Details and beside each to-do's ☆ (a category of one's own takes the picture its name calls for, like the brushes and mirror for Beauty, or keeps its emoji), and New Category offers the design's other categories ready-made. Speak a Task knows the new ones' words ("pay rent" goes to Finance, "dentist appointment" to Appointments). Each to-do on the list shows its title in bold navy, and its category's symbol sits on the right, just before ☆ ("Foot soak … 🌸 ☆ ✏️"): the category tabs at the top already say what each symbol means, so the name is not repeated, rows stay short and more to-dos fit on screen. A second, smaller line appears only when there is something to show: the time, steps done ("1 of 3"), 🔔 reminder, 🔁 repeat and the photo. In **Task Details**, ＋ **Add Step** sits right under the to-do's name, before Category: type a step and Return adds it (the field stays ready for the next one); tick a step with its circle, rewrite it in place or remove it with ✕, and the to-do's line on the list shows how many are done ("1 of 3"). A repeating to-do brings its steps along, unticked. The category chips are compact. 📷 **Photo**: take or choose a photo, add the title and details, and save; view it full size, replace it or remove it later. ▦ **Templates**: Morning Routine, Workout Routine, Travel Checklist, Study Session, Home Cleaning and Grocery Shopping, on a page from its design: a soft lilac heading with stars, hearts and clouds, a pink-edged **Close** and "Templates" in thick navy, then two columns of pastel cards, each starter with its own colour and picture (the sunrise, the weightlifting star, the plane and passport, the books, the broom and leaves, the trolley and grocery bag), its name in navy and "5 to-dos · Personal" in its colour, and a ⋯ in its corner; your own templates are pink with a **👑 My template** badge and little pictures that match what the list is about, read from its name and to-dos (📚📖🔖 for books and reading, 🍎🥕🍌 for fruit and vegetables, 🎂🎈🎁 for a party, 🧳🏖️🗺️ for a trip, 🐶🐱🦴 for pets, and about twenty more), or three pink hearts when nothing matches, and **Save as Template** is a dashed card with a big pink ＋ (the cards' pictures are the design's own, enlarged 4× with Real-ESRGAN; the words are written by the app). Open one and it works like the day's list: tick a to-do done (the two little hearts pop and the "ting" plays, the all-done chime for the last one) and it moves under **› Completed** with its count (tap to show or hide them; tap a finished one to untick it); "2 of 5 completed" sits at the top with **Untick All** to start the list again, and the card shows "2 of 5 done". The ticks are saved with the template. ＋ on a row adds just that to-do to My Day (it turns into a ✓ for a moment, and Templates stays open), and two buttons sit at the bottom: first ＋ **Add Task** (white with a pink edge), which opens a new row at the end of the list to type a to-do into the template (Return adds it and leaves the row ready for the next; Return on an empty row closes it), then **Add to My Day**, which adds every to-do not done yet. "Save as Template" keeps your own lists on the device (a name box, an emoji, a category, then ＋ **Add Task** to type the to-dos one by one; the field being typed in always stays in view above Save Template). Every template, the starters included, has a **⋯** button (and the same choices at the top of its list) to **Edit** it (name, emoji, category, and each to-do: rewrite, remove or add) or **Delete** it; deleted starters can be brought back with "Bring back the starter templates". **Press and hold** a template card to pick it up and move it around: the other cards make room as it passes over them, and the new order is kept (new templates go at the end; with VoiceOver, use the Move earlier and Move later actions). The first time Quick Add opens, a one-time tip from its design introduces it: the page takes a soft pink shade, Add Task, Add Priority and Add Journal glow among yellow sparkles, × shines with white rays, the yellow star with a pink bow peeks over a speech card ("**Quick Add ✨** Add a task, priority, or journal in one tap!", in the design's own lettering), and a soft pink arrow curls to Add Journal; the choices still work, and a tap anywhere else closes it. |

a photo, and your location only when you tap Use My Location. If one is turned off, My Day explains how to turn
it on in Settings and offers another way (for example, typing instead of speaking).

## Run it

1. Open `MyDay.xcodeproj` in Xcode.
2. Select the **MyDay** target → *Signing & Capabilities*. Choose your Team and,
   if needed, change the bundle identifier (`com.regina3579.myday`).
3. Pick an iPhone simulator or your iPhone, then press **Run** (⌘R).

### Set up iCloud sync

The project already has what SwiftData needs (Apple: "Syncing model data across a
person's devices"): the iCloud capability with CloudKit and the container
`iCloud.com.regina3579.myday`, Push Notifications (`MyDay/MyDay.entitlements`), and the
Remote notifications background mode (`MyDay/Info.plist`). Every model is CloudKit-ready: no
unique attributes, a default for every value, and optional relationships with inverses.

1. In *Signing & Capabilities*, under **iCloud**, make sure **CloudKit** and the container
   `iCloud.com.regina3579.myday` are ticked. A container name is unique across iCloud, so if
   Xcode can't create this one, add your own (`iCloud.` + your bundle identifier) and tick only
   that one; My Day uses the first container listed.
2. Sign in to iCloud on the iPhone or simulator, then run My Day from Xcode once with the launch
   argument `-initializeCloudKitSchema` (*Product → Scheme → Edit Scheme → Run → Arguments*). It
   writes every record type and field to the container's development schema.
3. Before you release the app, deploy the schema to production in
   [CloudKit Console](https://icloud.developer.apple.com) (*Deploy Schema Changes*). TestFlight
   and App Store builds use the production schema, and a production schema can only be added
   to: record types and fields can't be removed or renamed later.
4. To test: add a to-do, delete My Day, install it again and open it. Settings → iCloud shows
   "Syncing with iCloud…" and your data comes back (it takes from a few seconds to a few minutes,
   depending on the network).

### Set up ratings

Apple asks apps to rate through Apple's own pages (App Review Guideline 5.6.1: "Use the
provided API to prompt users to review your app"). My Day uses two of them:

- **With an App Store ID** (once My Day is in App Store Connect): put the number from My Day's
  App Store link (`apps.apple.com/app/id`**`1234567890`**) in `MyDay/Info.plist` →
  `MyDayAppStoreID`. **Rate Now** and Settings → **Rate My Day ⭐️** then open the App Store's
  *Write a Review* page (`https://apps.apple.com/app/id<ID>?action=write-review`), Apple's way to
  rate from a button.
- **Until then** they show Apple's in-app rating sheet (`RequestReviewAction`). Apple: it always
  shows in builds run from Xcode (you can't send a rating from them), never in TestFlight, and in
  the App Store at most 3 times in 365 days.

To see the card without waiting 7 days, a build run from Xcode has **Settings → Tips → Preview
the rating card** (a preview changes nothing).

### Set up My Day Pro

My Day Pro is an auto-renewable subscription, sold with StoreKit 2 and Apple's own subscription
sheet (`SubscriptionStoreView`, iOS 17), so prices, lengths, Restore Purchases and the terms
come from the App Store as Apple's guidelines ask (3.1.2: describe what the user gets for the
price; 3.1.1: a restore mechanism).

1. The Account Holder signs the **Paid Apps Agreement** in App Store Connect (*Business →
   Agreements*) and adds banking and tax details. Apple requires it to be Active before
   subscriptions can be offered, and even to test them in the sandbox
   ([Sign and update agreements](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-agreements/sign-and-update-agreements/)).
2. In [App Store Connect](https://appstoreconnect.apple.com), under My Day → *Subscriptions*,
   create a subscription group named **My Day Pro** with two subscriptions:
   `com.regina3579.myday.pro.monthly` (1 month) and `com.regina3579.myday.pro.yearly` (1 year),
   with their prices, a display name and a description each. To use other product IDs, change
   `MyDayPro.productIDs` (`MyDay/Services/MyDayPro.swift`).
3. Add the **In-App Purchase** capability in *Signing & Capabilities*, and a privacy policy URL
   in App Store Connect (the subscription sheet links to it; the terms link to Apple's standard
   licence agreement).
4. To test purchases from Xcode, create a StoreKit configuration file synced with App Store
   Connect (*File → New → File from Template → StoreKit Configuration File*) and choose it in
   *Product → Scheme → Edit Scheme → Run → Options → StoreKit Configuration*. Sandbox testers
   (App Store Connect → *Users and Access*) can buy on a device.
5. Without a subscription, a build run from Xcode has **Settings → My Day Pro → Pretend Pro
   (Debug)**: App Store, On or Off, to see every screen as a member or as a free account.

## How the home screen is built

Only the scenery is an image: `HomeScene` is the sky, castle and garden with
the girl, the puppy and the kitten. The old title, message and cards were
painted out of it. Everything on top is native SwiftUI: `HomeHeader`,
`DailyQuoteView`, three `HomeFeatureCard`s drawn with vector shapes, and
`QuickAddButton` + `QuickAddMenu`, with `BottomTabBar` from the root view.
Sizes follow the screen width, so the layout keeps the design's proportions
on every iPhone, and the screen scrolls when it does not fit (for example on
iPhone SE).

The To-Dos screen works the same way: only `TodosScene` (the sign, the girl and
her puppy) and `ProgressKitten` are images. The date card, chips, rows, buttons,
hearts and the "You're Doing Great!" badge are SwiftUI views.

The Calendar's `CalendarHeader` picture (the design's title, icon and Today button painted
out), its calendar icon, the flowers and the three row pictures are images; the text, the
buttons, the month grid and the rows are SwiftUI.

Today's Priority too: `PriorityScene` (the title, the girl, the puppy and the kitten,
with the design's buttons painted out) and `PriorityEmptyStar` are images; the add
field, the panel, the cards and the ⋮ menu are SwiftUI. The scene's top rows are soft
curtains at the top edge (behind the Dynamic Island), and the design's back and ⋮
buttons line up with the navigation bar's own.

The tab bar floats over the screens, so `RootView` publishes the height it
covers (`tabBarClearance`) and every screen keeps its content clear of it with
`tabBarSafeArea()`. Full-screen pages (Today's Priority) hide it instead, with
`Router.setFullScreen(_:in:)`.

## Continuous integration

`.github/workflows/ios-build.yml` builds the app for the iOS Simulator on every
push and pull request. It then runs `scripts/screenshots.sh`, which opens every
main screen in the iPhone simulators and uploads the screenshots as a build
artifact. Running the workflow by hand with *publish_screenshots* also commits small
JPEG copies to `docs/screenshots/`. You can run the script on a Mac too:
`./scripts/screenshots.sh`.
